BUDGET ALLOCATION

The next dollar is not the average dollar

Why average ROAS and marginal ROAS answer different questions, and what that means for marketing budget decisions.

Knomatrix perspective · 4-minute read

Two ratios. Two decisions.

Average ROAS divides the outcome attributed to a channel by its spend over a defined period. Marginal ROAS asks how much the outcome changes with a small change in spend. A channel can have an attractive average return while offering a weaker return on additional investment.

Diminishing returns explain the difference.

As spending increases, the next impression or customer may be harder to reach. A response curve makes that assumption explicit. In the lab’s simple function, response = A × spend / (K + spend), the derivative is A × K / (K + spend)². The marginal response declines as spend rises.

Compare consistent economics.

Revenue ROAS and contribution return are different measures. Before ranking channels, align the outcome, costs, customer maturity and time horizon. A high short-term acquisition return may not translate into strong mature-cohort value.

Do not extrapolate a local estimate indefinitely.

Multiplying a small-change marginal return by a large budget shift is a first-pass approximation. A full scenario should evaluate the response curve at the new allocation and respect operating constraints. The resulting estimate still needs validation and uncertainty.

The practical decision.

Use average returns to understand the existing allocation. Use marginal response and feasible scenarios to challenge the next allocation. Use experiments where the historical data cannot support the distinction.
