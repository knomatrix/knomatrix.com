COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE

A competitor benchmark is a question, not an answer

How to use search, app, social and website signals without confusing visibility with business performance.

Knomatrix perspective · 4-minute read

Start with a comparable measurement.

Search interest, keyword counts, estimated visits and app downloads are different signals. Their definitions, geography, period and provider coverage must match before comparing operators. A larger number is not automatically evidence of a stronger business.

Visibility does not equal value.

A competitor may appear more often on non-brand searches while attracting lower-value traffic. An app may receive more installs without retaining more paying customers. These observations can identify gaps, but they do not reveal a competitor’s financial outcomes.

Connect outside-in and inside-out evidence.

External signals are most useful when they guide questions for your first-party data. If non-brand visibility is low, investigate qualified conversion and unit economics. If mobile performance is weak, inspect funnel steps and design a controlled test.

Keep a hypothesis register.

For each gap, record the source, the metric definition, the likely mechanism, the business outcome and the evidence that could disprove the hypothesis. This turns a benchmark deck into a prioritised work programme.

Measure the decision, not just the observation.

The endpoint is a testable action: improve a page, investigate a cohort, defend a query cluster or stage a budget change. Revisit the outcome so the next diagnostic contains more evidence and fewer assumptions.
