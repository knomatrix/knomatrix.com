THE QUESTION BEHIND THE MODEL

What would change
if the mix changed?

Marketing mix modelling uses variation in business outcomes, media and relevant controls to estimate how activity relates to performance over time. Carryover and saturation represent delayed effects and diminishing returns.

We start with the business outcome and the decision horizon. A revenue model, a contribution model and an acquisition model answer different questions. Their definitions should be explicit before the result reaches the budget meeting.

Confidence comes
from challenge.

A strong model fit does not prove that every channel estimate is correct. We inspect out-of-time performance, residuals, channel correlation, parameter stability and business plausibility.

Recommendations should carry their assumptions. Where the historical data cannot separate effects, the next step may be a controlled experiment rather than a more elaborate model.
